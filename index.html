Know your machine

Understanding Your Washing Machine

There are three types on the market — and they are not the same. Know yours before you do anything else.

Front loader

Uses a pull-out tray that auto-doses each part of the cycle. Easiest to use, nightmare to keep mold-free.

💡 Leave the door open after every wash. Wipe the rubber seal. You will regret it if you don't.

Top loader — agitator

The center column "agitates" your clothes using friction. Don't overstuff it or nothing gets clean.

💡 Fill to 75% max. Overfilling = clothes flopping around without actually getting clean.

Top loader — impeller

A ridged plate at the bottom rotates clothes. Requires a very specific loading technique.

💡 Load clothes in a donut shape — hole in the middle. I know it sounds weird. Trust the donut.

The universal 75% rule

75% clothes
25% air ←

Quick test: make a fist above your clothes. If your knuckles touch the top of the drum, you're about right.

75% is your standard normal load. Smaller loads are totally fine — just dose your detergent and boosters down proportionally.

Machine care

Maintaining Your Machine

Clothes cannot get clean in a dirty washer. This is not up for debate. Clean it every ~90 loads — roughly once or twice a quarter.

"SCRUD" is a real word for the black gunky dots that appear when you finally clean a neglected machine. You're welcome for that knowledge.

🍋 Regular maintenance

Add ½ cup of pure citric acid powder to the drum and run the longest, hottest cycle. Repeat until the water runs clean. Cheap, effective, deeply satisfying.

🦠 If you have buildup / SCRUD

Run several citric acid cycles until the water clears. Wipe away any gross black SCRUD as it comes loose. Clean your detergent drawer and lint trap with an antibacterial like Microban. Google your specific machine model to find the filter.

Not a DIY person? Fair enough. There are several solid washing machine cleaners that do the same job — they all contain citric acid plus a few extra components to go the extra mile. I like Affresh Tabs, Truly Free Washing Machine Cleaner (yes, their laundry detergent is on my avoid list — their machine cleaner is a completely different story and it's genuinely excellent), FEBU Washing Machine Cleaner, and Renuv Heavy Duty Washing Machine Tablets for seriously neglected machines.

Water science (yes, really)

Understanding Water Hardness

Look up your level in your city's municipal water report — a quick Google will find it. On well water? Test it with the API Fish Kit — you almost certainly have hard water.

0 ppm — Soft100 ppm200 ppm400+ ppm — Very hard
Soft
0–100 ppm
You lucky duck. No softening needed. Standard detergent dose works perfectly.
Moderately hard
100–200 ppm
Add 1–2 tsp washing soda or borax to the drum. Actually lets you use less detergent — saving money!
Hard
200–400 ppm
Add 2 tbsp washing soda + 1 tbsp citric acid to the drum. Binds mineral deposits so your detergent can actually do its job.
Very hard
400+ ppm
Add 4 tbsp washing soda + 2 tbsp citric acid per load. Or 1 tsp Trisodium Phosphate — less eco-friendly, your call.
💡 Want an easier way to soften your water? There's a product called Calgon that makes dosing simple — just add it to your load and it handles the softening for you. The catch: you sometimes need to use quite a bit of it, which can get pricey. But it works well and is a great option if you don't want to mess with washing soda and citric acid separately.
✦ How to know you got the dose right: Look for "trace suds" — a thin film of 3–4 tiny bubbles on the window (front loader) or on the water surface (top loader) about 10 minutes in. That's the sweet spot. If you're seeing a bubble party, you've overdone it. And if your items are coming out of the dryer a bit stiff, just down-dose your water softeners until you reach your desired outcome.

Detergent deep dive

Types of Detergents, Enzymes & Boosters

Choosing your detergent is personal. Eczema kid? Nostalgic for Tide? We hear you. But let's talk about what's actually in there first.

Laundry sheets are trash and pods are for college freshmen. You're an adult now. Time to level up.

What are surfactants? Short for "surface-acting agents," surfactants are the workhorses of your detergent. They connect soils to water — even oily or sticky soils that would cling to fabric — so everything can rinse away. They work best when paired with enzymes.

💧 Liquid detergent

Great surfactant package but works better paired with an enzymatic powder booster. Max dose: roughly 4 tbsp. That combo gives you the full spectrum of cleaning power.

🧂 Powder detergent

The overachiever — surfactants, enzymes, AND oxygen bleach in one. Sweet spot is 2–3 heaping tbsp in the drum. Pair with a booster on extra dirty loads.

⚡ Boosters (OxiClean, BIZ, FEBU)

Boosters add extra enzymes, stain fighters, and water softeners to supercharge your detergent. Honestly, you can just buy a tub of sodium percarbonate (the active ingredient in all of them) and use that. I use FEBU for adult clothes and BIZ for the kids' stuff — because I'm not made of money.

Back to high school science

The Enzymes You Need to Know

Each enzyme targets a different type of stain. Most powder detergents contain the first six. DNase is newer — and if you wear leggings or have teenage boys, it's going to change your life.

Protease
Breaks down proteins
Blood, grass, body soil, collar grime
⚠ Don't use on wool, cashmere, or linen
Lipase
Dissolves fats & oils
Cooking oil, sebum, body oils, pit stains. Lipase is that girl. Essential if you have a toddler.
Amylase
Breaks down starches
Pasta sauce, food stains, baby formula
Cellulase
Manages fabric fibers
Trims broken microfibrils that make colors look faded and cause pilling
Mannanase
Dissolves gum-based residues
Cosmetics, ice cream, certain food thickeners
Pectate lyase
Handles fruit & wine pigments
Berry stains, red wine, fruit juice
✨ New to market
DNase
Breaks down deep organic deposits
Odor-causing residue trapped deep in synthetic fabrics — this is why gym clothes still smell after washing. Your detergent cleans the surface. DNase reaches what it can't.

Pairing a liquid detergent with FEBU covers the full enzyme spectrum. If you use powder with enzymes, adding a DNase-specific product like Gear Guard is your finishing move.

I try to be a scrunchy mom. Glass containers, organic produce, the whole thing. But I draw the line at laundry. Your standard "non-toxic" detergent is not cleaning your clothes. I tried. Molly's Suds belongs under your sink next to the toilet brush.
